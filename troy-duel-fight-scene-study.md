**Scope + IP note:** this is a craft study of *shot grammar* (cut rhythm, camera height,
motion, sound placement) from copyrighted studio footage. We reverse-engineer the GRAMMAR
and rebuild an original scene with our own character and story. We never replicate the
footage, the choreography beat-for-beat, or any line of the film's dialogue.

## 2. THE TWELVE LAWS THIS SEQUENCE RUNS ON

**L1 — Average shot length 1.05 seconds. Nothing is allowed to hold.**
20 cuts in 22.0s. Longest shot 1.8s, shortest 0.8s. The 1.8s outlier is not an accident —
it is the ONE shot that has to teach the audience the geography, so it is bought extra time.

**L2 — Zero dialogue for 22 straight seconds.**
Not one spoken line. Only combat grunts ("Ugh!", "Argh!", "Yah!"). Every bit of story is
carried by picture and sound. This is the single biggest structural difference from our
channel's normal narrated format.

**L3 — Low angle is the default; eye-level is the exception.**
11 of 21 shots sit low-angle or worm's-eye; eye-level appears mainly on the *even* exchanges
(4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 18). High angle: never. Low angle makes both fighters monumental —
the moment you go eye-level, you are saying "these two are matched right now."

**L4 — Every single cut lands on motion.** Hard cut on forward motion, cut-on-strike,
match cut on spin, cut-on-action. Not one cut in 22s lands on a still frame.

**L5 — The camera is almost never locked.** Only two near-static shots (12 and 16), and both
are deployed as deliberate micro-pauses inside the churn — a breath, then a plot turn.

**L6 — Establish once, re-establish once, then live in tight.**
Shot 4 (1.8s, full shot, walls + crowd) establishes. Shot 8 (0.8s, whip up to the parapet)
re-establishes and reminds you thousands are watching. Everything else is MS or tighter.

**L7 — Strict attacker/defender alternation.** Achilles shots and Hector shots trade off so
the viewer never loses track of who is winning. Achilles gets the airborne, spinning,
advancing, leaping shots; Hector gets the dodging, recovering, falling, flailing shots.
Dominance is expressed through *which verbs each man's shots contain*.

**L8 — Shot size tightens as the fight turns.** MS/FS at the open → CU by 0:05 → ECU exactly
at the collapse (shot 15). The frame closes in as the outcome closes in.

**L9 — The plot turn is a 0.9-second static macro insert of a foot on a rock.**
The entire dramatic reversal — the hero loses not to skill but to the ground — is carried by
one worm's-eye insert of loose stone. Smallest shot in the sequence, biggest job.

**L10 — Sound is doing half the work, and it is all diegetic.**
Impacts, clangs, whooshes, sand foley, breath, distant crowd. Score is only a low orchestral
drone *underneath* — it never takes the front. Every cut has a physical sound event on it.

**L11 — One grade throughout: hard high sun, warm sand, high contrast, dust in the air.**
No grade shifts, no filtration changes. Consistency is what lets 21 shots read as one event.

**L12 — Structure: hook / clash / turn / desperation.**
- 0:00–0:02 **Hook** — in media res, mid-air spear attack. No setup, no context, no title.
- 0:02–0:11 **Clash** — sustained exchange, Achilles' dominance established.
- 0:11–0:16 **Turn** — distance breaks, footing lost on stone.
- 0:16–0:22 **Desperation** — grounded flailing met with contemptuous ease.

**The hook, precisely:** the first 1.5 seconds give you a charge and then a man in the air with
a spear. It works because it is pure *unresolved kinetic energy* — the spear has not landed
yet, so you physically cannot leave until it does.

## 3. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR AN AI RECREATION

**The trap: 21 shots ≠ 21 generations.** Seedance clips are multi-second; a 1-second
generation is neither available nor sensible, and one generation cannot contain four
different camera angles.

**The method that actually works:** generate **10–12 clips at ~4–5s each**, each holding ONE
clean action beat with ONE camera move (per this project's one-move-camera doctrine), then in
the edit **harvest the best 1–1.5s window from each** — two windows from the clips that
sustain — and assemble 21 beats with ffmpeg, cutting on motion per L4. Roughly 12 generations
fill a 22-second sequence. This is also far cheaper than chasing 21.

**Known constraints from our own logs that apply directly here:**
- `PROMPT_LEARNINGS.md` **S12** — never a dead-on head-on angle for anything that must read
  with depth; use an oblique offset. Relevant to every charge/advance shot.
- The **maximum-realism lock** — a full arena crowd is exactly the failure mode logged on the
  Escobar Congress shot (every background figure identically dressed). Crowd variation has to
  be written into the prompt explicitly, every time.
- The **era-accuracy lock** — applies to every person in the stands, not just the fighters.
- The **identity lock** must survive helmet, armor, dust, motion blur and fast handheld — the
  hardest possible conditions for Hazel's face to hold.
