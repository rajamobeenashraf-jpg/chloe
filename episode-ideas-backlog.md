## Twelve episode ideas (Ep 5+)

1. **"I Tried to Warn Pompeii" (79 AD)** — On-screen countdown to eruption over an ordinary market day. *Active role:* she can't save the city, so she spends the episode saving ONE family — arguing with the father, grabbing the kid's hand, dragging them toward the harbor as ash starts falling. *Why:* the niche's two biggest videos ever are doomed-setting stories; Chloe's Pompeii Short did 884k and there's still no definitive treatment. Ends on the modern casts, memorial tone.

2. **"I Boarded the Hindenburg's Last Flight" (1937)** — *Active role:* she works the flight as a stewardess (jobs subformat), and during the final descent quietly moves passengers toward the observation windows — where the real survivors got out. 62 of 97 lived; she can nudge the odds. *Why:* Titanic-formula transfer, US crash site, completely untouched in the niche.

3. **"The Night Chicago Burned" (1871)** — *Active role:* wakes a boarding house door by door, joins the bucket brigade, leads a group to the lake as the fire jumps the river. *Why:* disaster-eve + major US city + fire at night is spectacular AI material. Irony beat: everyone's already blaming the cow — she knows the legend is false (pinned-comment gold).

4. **"I Arrived in America With Nothing" (Ellis Island, 1907)** — *Active role:* goes through processing herself (medical line, chalk marks, the legal questions), then carries bags and vouches for a family about to be separated. *Why:* the most-quoted beat type in the whole genre is ordinary people's hopes — this is an entire episode of it. The quiet, prestige entry.

5. **"I Got Into a Speakeasy — Then It Got Raided" (NYC, 1926)** — *Active role:* password at the door, then when the whistles blow she's stuffing the ledger behind the bar panel and pushing the band out the coal chute with their instruments. *Why:* the single most AI-flattering aesthetic available (neon, rain, jazz, art deco); Chloe's 1920s Short did 457k. Music must be AI-composed period-style, nothing licensed.

6. **"Black Sunday" (Dust Bowl, April 14 1935)** — *Active role:* on a Panhandle farm when the black wall appears — wet sheets on the windows, rope line to the barn, then back OUT on the rope for the kid caught in the field. *Why:* survival here is preparation, not sprinting — the anti-clip-8 by design. Haunting, low-artifact visuals; Grapes-of-Wrath curriculum tie-in.

7. **"I Sailed to Troy" (Ancient Greece)** — *Active role:* drafted as a water-bearer in the Greek camp, patches a soldier's shield arm, sneaks to the walls during a truce. *Why:* Trojan War searches +529%, Agamemnon +1,757% (Odyssey-movie halo) — ride the wave. Myth-vs-reality engine; "was there even a horse?" is built-in comment bait.

8. **"The City Cortés Destroyed — Two Years Before He Arrived" (Tenochtitlan, 1519)** — *Active role:* market day — pays in cacao beans, poles a farmer's produce canoe down a canal, climbs the great pyramid. *Why:* Chloe's Aztec Short hit 1.01M with her highest-ever comment rate. A love letter to a city the audience only knows as ruins; the irony is unbearable in the best way.

9. **"The Day Boston Drowned in Molasses" (Jan 15, 1919)** — *Active role:* hears the tank groaning (she knows), fails to get the foreman to listen (the sheriff-beat echo from Ep 1), then hauls people onto a cart roof as a 25-foot wave of molasses takes the street. *Why:* "weird history" is a 60k/mo US search pool, the absurd-to-horrifying whiplash is exactly this channel's grammar, and NOBODY has made it. 21 real deaths keep it grounded — memorial-tone pinned comment.

10. **"18 Hours Before the Earthquake" (San Francisco, April 17–18 1906)** — *Active role:* backstage helper at the opera house (Caruso really sang Carmen that night), then at 5:12am she's pulling boarding-house neighbors into doorframes and out to the street, joining bucket lines as the fires start. *Why:* disaster-eve countdown structure, major US city, untouched.

11. **"I Was a Maid in a Gilded Age Mansion" (Newport, 1895)** — *Active role:* she DOES the job — 5am coal grates, the below-stairs hierarchy, caught sitting down by the housekeeper, serves a dinner where one course costs her yearly wage. *Why:* Chloe's maid Short did 308k and the jobs subformat consistently over-indexes; strong female-skewing audience; American twist on her UK-heavy lane.

12. **"The Golden Spike — and the Men They Cropped Out of the Photo" (Promontory, 1869)** — *Active role:* works water and tools alongside a Chinese track crew in the final miles. **The universe move: the crew foreman is Li — from Episode 4, twenty years later.** First cross-episode character return; the comment section will detonate. *Why:* irony beat with real teeth — the Chinese workers were left out of the famous photograph, and she makes sure the camera remembers them. Community-integrated continuity is one of the seven load-bearing mechanics; this is the strongest possible version of it.

### Sequencing & seasonal notes
- Keep alternating centuries/looks — never two adjacent episodes with the same palette (the Ep 1–4 rule, continued).
- Anniversary releases when convenient: Molasses = Jan 15 · SF quake = Apr 18 · Black Sunday = Apr 14 · Hindenburg = May 6 · Chicago Fire = Oct 8 (pairs with Salem-season October).
- **Li's return (#12) should air 2–3 episodes after Gold Rush** so viewers still remember him — that gap is what makes the payoff land.
- Handle-with-care list: any war/atrocity setting gets the Titanic memorial playbook (researched pinned comment, no comedy in the casualty beats) or doesn't get made as a Short.
