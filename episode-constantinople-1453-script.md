## Fact checklist (script-stage verification per craft-study §2 — all claims in dialogue)
- Siege of 1453: April 6 – May 29 (53 days); final assault at dawn, May 29. ✔
- Golden Horn chain: real; the harbor boom across to Galata. ✔
- Ships overland: night of April 21–22; ~70 ships (sources 67–80 — dialogue says "seventy") dragged over the Galata hill on greased log rollers into the Golden Horn. ✔
- Giovanni Giustiniani Longo: Genoese, arrived Jan 1453 with ~700 men, commanded the land-wall defense, wounded during the final assault, evacuated to the ships, died of the wound weeks later. His removal is widely recorded as the morale break. ✔ (his death is post-episode; not claimed in dialogue)
- Orban's bombard: stone balls ~1,200 lb; rate of fire ~7 shots/day (dialogue's "one shot every three hours" ≈ that rate). ✔ ("biggest cannon ever built" — matches her claim; pinned comment can carry nuance)
- "Land walls never breached in 1,100 years": the THEODOSIAN LAND WALLS were never taken by force before 1453 — dialogue deliberately says "these land walls" because the 1204 Crusader sack entered via the sea walls. Pinned comment states 1204 plainly. ✔
- Constantine XI: last emperor; removed imperial insignia and died in the final fighting; body never reliably identified. ✔
- Angel legend at Hagia Sophia: recorded contemporary belief. ✔
- Mehmed II: age 21 at the conquest; the halted-looter anecdote (soldier breaking the marble pavement, Mehmed stopping him — "the buildings are mine") is from the near-contemporary accounts (Doukas tradition); dialogue frames it as what happens, pinned comment cites it. Hagia Sophia converted to a mosque immediately (first Friday prayers within days). ✔
- "You can walk under it today": Hagia Sophia stands; dialogue avoids claiming its current status beyond standing/visitable. ✔
- NOT depicted, owned in the pinned comment: the sack and enslavement of the population (with care, per the handle-with-care rule), the 1204 nuance, Morea/Trebizond surviving to 1460/61 (the "last piece of Rome" correction her video took), Giustiniani's later death, the city's name history (officially Istanbul in 1930).
