## EPISODE 3 — "The Night Paul Revere Rode" (Boston, 1775)
**Runtime ~125s · 12 clips · Recurring character: Robert Newman (real historical sexton)**

| # | Dur | Scene & sample lines | Active-participant job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7s | Cold open: night gallop, torchlight strobing — "Okay, nobody mentioned the revolution would involve ME on a horse—" cut. | Hook = participation |
| 2 | 9s | Rewind/plan: daytime harbor. "April 18th, 1775. Tonight, somewhere in this city, the most famous ride in American history happens. I am not supposed to be anywhere near it." | Irony clock starts |
| 3 | 9s | Costume + Green Dragon Tavern, orders ale: "Warm. Flat. Extremely 1775." | Sensory beat |
| 4 | 11s | Leans into a hushed Sons of Liberty argument at the next table, inserts herself: "Which road — Lexington or Concord?" / "Who's asking?" | She joins the conversation |
| 5 | 7s | A name lands — "Revere." Her face changes. "Oh no. It's tonight." | Recognition beat |
| 6 | 10s | Old North Church side door: sexton Robert Newman struggling with a heavy lantern crate — she grabs the other handle before he can ask. "You're short-handed. I'm free." | She volunteers into the event |
| 7 | 13s | Tower stairs together — narrow, dark, both breathing hard, lanterns swinging. Hang them side by side. "One if by land—" / "—two if by sea. I know how this part goes." | Physical teamwork in the actual signal |
| 8 | 12s | British sentry below — she steps out FIRST, plants herself in his path, talks fast: "Evening — just checking the tower's sound, my father's the verger, terrible acoustics—" walks him backward while Newman slips out behind. | Social engineering, not hiding |
| 9 | 13s | On horseback, side lane — she reaches a farmhouse ahead of the riders and pounds the door herself: "The regulars are coming out — get your musket, ring your bell!" A woman grabs a gun; a boy bolts for the bell rope. | She personally delivers the warning |
| 10 | 14s | A patrol spots her — cuts off-road, jumps a low stone wall, hauls a hay cart sideways across the lane behind her, dives into a barn. | Terrain + objects to escape — never a flat sprint |
| 11 | 11s | Dawn, hilltop, Lexington green distant — faint crack of the first shots. She goes still, unguarded. "Down there, right now, nobody knows they're standing in the first ten seconds of a country." | Awe beat, emotional core |
| 12 | 9s | Reflective outro, ritual line, tease: "If you want to know what happens on that green next — tell me." Cut to black. | Soft series hook |

**Pinned comment:** Robert Newman was the real sexton who hung the lanterns (arrested, later released) — cite him; "shot heard round the world" is Emerson, written decades later, not a contemporary phrase; standard disclosure line.

## EPISODE 4 — "I Joined the Gold Rush of 1849" (Sierra foothills, California)
**Runtime ~132s · 12 clips · Recurring characters: Amos (free Black miner) and Li (Chinese miner) — her rocker crew**

**Anti-rerun design vs Ep 1 (deliberate):** no town, no saloon, no dusty street. Sierra river canyon — pines, snowmelt river, mud, rain light, canvas tents. Cold green/grey palette vs 1875's warm amber. Wardrobe: mud-caked flannel + work boots + slouch hat vs the corset dress. Antagonists: greed, disease, and the river — not robbers.

| # | Dur | Scene & sample lines | Active-participant job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7s | Cold open: night, torches, mob roaring "Rope 'em!" — she shoves through to the FRONT, arms out: "Wait— WAIT—" smash cut. | Hook = intervening in a hanging vote |
| 2 | 9s | Rewind/plan: ridge over tent-dotted river canyon. "California, 1849. A hundred thousand people came for gold. Most go home with nothing. My plan: don't get rich. Just don't get robbed." | Irony clock starts |
| 3 | 11s | Camp store: she buys ONE egg. "That's a dollar. For an egg. That's a week's wages back east... I'm buying it. 9/10 — tastes like financial ruin." | Food beat via real 1849 prices |
| 4 | 8s | Work-kit change: flannel, boots, hat, instantly mud to the knees. "Fashion in the diggings is: alive, and dry from the waist up. I'm one for two." | Costume ritual, new silhouette |
| 5 | 12s | She joins Amos & Li's rocker crew uninvited — steps in on the cradle handle, matches rhythm, earns the nod. "Shake it like—" / "—a cradle. Baby analogy. Got it." | She works, doesn't watch |
| 6 | 10s | First color: flakes in the riffle; camp erupts over a strike upstream. Flake to lens: "Eleven cents. People are dying out here eleven cents at a time." Whisper: "The men selling the shovels are the ones getting rich. I can't tell them that." | Irony beat |
| 7 | 13s | Campfire core: "What's the gold for?" Answers round the ring. Amos: buying his wife's freedom back in Missouri — exact price memorized. She goes completely quiet. | The Titanic-steerage empathy beat |
| 8 | 12s | Cholera scare: young miner collapses; camp blames "bad air." She moves — shade, boils river water, makes him drink only that. Whisper: "It's in the water. Third trip in a row I've hit a germ-theory problem I'm not allowed to explain." | Acts on knowledge she can't reveal |
| 9 | 12s | Claim jump: two men ripping Li's stakes out, shoving him into the shallows. She grabs the camp's iron alarm triangle and hammers it: "CLAIM JUMP! MEETING — NOW!" | Weaponizes the camp's own institution |
| 10 | 14s | Miners' court: she testifies, points out boundary markers, quotes the district code — "He staked it, he worked it. Your code says it's his." Vote for Li. Mob heat spikes — "Rope 'em!" — she argues it DOWN: "You're a court, not a mob. Banish them." | Pays off the cold open — she stops the hanging |
| 11 | 13s | River surge: rain upstream, river jumps its banks — cradle and Amos's pouch go in. She wades to the eddy, hooks the pouch with a shovel handle while Li anchors her wrist. Gasping: "That bag is a PERSON'S FREEDOM. It does not go in the river." | Set-piece solved with objects + teamwork |
| 12 | 11s | Outro, dusk: presses her own flakes into Amos's hand, walks the ridge. "Everyone came here to strike it rich. The richest thing I saw was a man saving eleven cents at a time for someone he loves. Back to 2026 — follow for the next trip." | Reflection lands on beat 7 |

**Structural note:** clips 7→11 chain — the campfire gives the pouch its meaning, so the river set-piece is an emotional payoff, not just spectacle.

**Pinned comment:** documented 1849 prices (eggs $1–3, flour by the dollar-a-pound); miners' courts/district codes were the camps' real law, banishment a common sentence; free and enslaved Black miners genuinely purchased their own and family members' freedom with gold-rush earnings (documented cases); cholera swept the camps 1849–50; merchants (Brannan, later Strauss) out-earned virtually every digger. Standard disclosure line.
