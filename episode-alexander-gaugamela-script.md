## v5 change: the battle brought closer, told as a real sequence (owner instruction)

Owner correction on v4: the battle needed to be closer to the action, AND told as proper storytelling — no cutting straight to an abstract dust-cloud for the sake of the runtime. His explicit bar: **a viewer watching on mute should be able to follow what actually happened.** v4's single 13s "charge" clip was one abstracted wide shot; it didn't clear that bar. v5 replaces it with **three clips forming one continuous, visually legible sequence**, and moves her physically INTO the advancing support line behind the charge instead of watching from a rise — closer, but still never a combatant (unchanged rule).

**The three-clip battle sequence, each clip staged so the cause-and-effect reads without audio:**
1. **The wedge forms and moves** — the cavalry visibly grouped into a spearhead shape with Alexander at its point, the gap in the Persian line visible ahead of them as a real target, not implied off-screen. She's swept forward with the hypaspists and phalanx battalions following up behind the cavalry (historically accurate — they went through the same gap) — visibly closing distance clip to clip, not teleporting from "safe rise" to "in the chaos."
2. **The impact** — the wedge visibly driving into the gap, the Persian line specifically shown buckling/scattering at the point of contact (shields dropping, spears turning), not a generic dust blur. This is the shot that has to read as "the line broke HERE" with the sound off.
3. **Darius flees, and it ripples** — his chariot and standard staged as visually distinct from the rest of the line so a muted viewer can identify him, turning and fleeing; nearby Persian ranks are shown looking toward the fleeing chariot BEFORE they break and run themselves — the domino effect visible as a sequence of glances and reactions, not stated in dialogue. She's now close enough to be caught at the edge of that collapse.

**One new verified fact anchors clip 3 of the sequence:** Darius genuinely did turn his own chariot and flee once the Macedonian wedge broke through — confirmed across multiple sources — and that flight, not the fighting itself, is what actually broke Persian morale and decided the battle. This is a stronger, more accurate climax than v4's abstracted version: the battle is WON at the moment people see their king run, which is exactly the kind of thing that reads clearly with no sound.

**Checked and deliberately NOT used:** the famous "Alexander Mosaic" (Pompeii, House of the Faun) depicting Alexander charging at a fleeing Darius in a chariot — it's the obvious nerd-spike to reach for here, but scholarly attribution is genuinely split between this battle and Issus (333 BC), not settled. Left out rather than risk citing it confidently as "this exact moment."

**Pinned comment:** Gaugamela (Oct 1, 331 BC), near modern Erbil, Iraq. Alexander oversleeping the morning of the battle and Parmenion waking him is recorded by Plutarch — honestly flagged: a contemporary Babylonian astronomical diary suggests the Macedonians actually attacked early that day, so this may be later embellishment rather than fact. "I will not steal a victory" is Arrian's version of his reply to Parmenion's night-attack proposal — some historians think the line may have been added later for propaganda value. The Iliad of the Casket (Aristotle's edited copy, kept under Alexander's pillow with his dagger) is from Plutarch, citing Onesicritus. Alexander really did lead the Companion cavalry personally into a gap that opened in the Persian center. Darius really did turn his own chariot and flee once that gap broke through, and that flight — not the fighting itself — is what actually collapsed Persian morale and decided the battle. Darius III's mother, wife, and two daughters were captured at Issus in 333 BC and remained in Alexander's camp, treated respectfully, at the time of Gaugamela. Alexander died in Babylon in 323 BC, aged 32. Parmenion is real; the specific words given to him and to Alexander in clip 2 are the dramatized version of what ancient sources report, not a transcript. AI-disclosure line + follow CTA.
