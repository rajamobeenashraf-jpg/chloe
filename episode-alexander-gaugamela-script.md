## v4 change: refocused onto ALEXANDER as the person, not just the event (owner instruction)

Owner correction on v3: he wants the FOCUS to be Alexander himself — v3 had him in exactly one clip (the earned Ep-9-Khufu-style cameo), which was too little. He also wants the event kept, not dropped. v4 restructures around that: **Alexander is now on screen in 6 of 8 clips**, most of it real two-way dialogue, closer to the Cleopatra "she tours with the person" pattern from this session's teardown than the Vikings/Aztecs "event with one hinge line" pattern. The event (the battle itself, and specifically his own cavalry charge) is kept as the mid-episode set-piece, not cut — it's just now framed as something SHE WATCHES HIM DO, not something that happens while he's elsewhere.

**New device for this to work: her role changed from camp medic to a junior scribe/runner attached directly to Alexander for the day** — same active-participant logic (§12: she has a real job, not tourist status), but a role that puts her physically next to him instead of in a medical tent. This is the structural fix that makes person-focus possible within 60–90s: no separate "her" storyline and "his" storyline to cut between — one storyline, him at the center of it, matching what was asked.

**Four new facts, all verified this session (WebSearch), replace most of v3's content:**
1. **Alexander overslept the morning of his biggest battle** — Plutarch: his officers found him still deeply asleep at dawn; Parmenion had to call his name two or three times to wake him, "asking how he could sleep so long like one who had already overcome." *(Honest caveat, disclosed in the pinned comment: a contemporary Babylonian astronomical-diary record contradicts this and suggests the Macedonians actually attacked early — the oversleeping story may be later Greek embellishment. Used anyway, flagged as tradition, the same way Ep 6 flags "Et tu, Brute" as Shakespeare not history.)*
2. **"I will not steal a victory"** — Arrian: the night before, Parmenion urged a night attack given Darius's numbers; Alexander refused, saying he wouldn't steal a victory, and that Darius's defeat had to be open, without stratagem. (Also flagged honestly: some historians think this line may have been assigned to him later for its propaganda value — noted in the pinned comment, not hidden.) Compressed into the same dawn scene as fact 1 to keep the episode's single linear day intact rather than depicting it as a separate "night before" scene.
3. **The Iliad of the Casket** — Plutarch (citing Onesicritus): Alexander carried Aristotle's own edited copy of the Iliad, called this his military "viaticum" (survival kit), and kept it with his dagger under his pillow — when offered the most precious object in Darius's captured treasury (a jeweled coffer), he chose to store the Iliad in it.
4. **Bucephalus at Gaugamela** — his warhorse, which only he could ride, carried him into this exact battle (one of several, before Bucephalus's death at the Hydaspes five years later — not conflated here).

**Carried over from v2/v3, kept but shortened to a single aside line (no longer its own clip):** Darius's mother, wife, and children were captured at Issus (333 BC) and were still in Alexander's camp at Gaugamela — same source as before, now delivered as one line while walking rather than a dedicated scene, to protect runtime for the new Alexander-centric material.

**Cut in v4 to make room:** Nikias, Straton, Bias, and the medical-tent/greave-strap/baggage-camp-raid material from v2/v3 are gone — that whole storyline existed to justify her proximity to the danger while Alexander was elsewhere; with him now central, it's no longer needed. The battle itself survives as his cavalry charge, not the baggage-camp raid. **New minor running gag** replaces the greave strap: her wax-tablet stylus, fumbled throughout, finally held steady by the outro.

**Pinned comment:** Gaugamela (Oct 1, 331 BC), near modern Erbil, Iraq. Alexander oversleeping the morning of the battle and Parmenion waking him is recorded by Plutarch — worth knowing honestly: a contemporary Babylonian astronomical diary suggests the Macedonians actually attacked early that day, so this may be a later embellished story rather than fact; included here as the traditional account, not confirmed history. "I will not steal a victory" is Arrian's version of Alexander's reply to Parmenion's night-attack proposal — some historians think the line may have been added later for its propaganda value. The Iliad of the Casket (Aristotle's edited copy, kept under Alexander's pillow with his dagger) is from Plutarch, citing the historian Onesicritus. Alexander really did lead the Companion cavalry personally into a gap that opened in the Persian center, aiming directly for Darius, who fled — the moment that decided the battle. Darius III's mother, wife, and two daughters were captured at Issus in 333 BC and remained in Alexander's camp, treated respectfully, at the time of Gaugamela. Alexander died in Babylon in 323 BC, aged 32. Parmenion is real; the specific words given to him and to Alexander in clip 2 are the dramatized version of what ancient sources report, not a transcript. AI-disclosure line + follow CTA.
