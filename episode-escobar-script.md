## §32 NPC locks (write once, reuse VERBATIM in every prompt where they appear)

**PABLO ESCOBAR** — 37 years old; stocky build, short (about 5'6"); thick black
curly hair, side-parted; full dark mustache; round, soft-featured face with a double
chin; heavy-lidded, unhurried eyes; faint permanent half-smile. Wears an
open-collared short-sleeve shirt (pale blue), dark casual slacks, white sneakers.
He never raises his voice and never hurries — every word soft-spoken, warm, and
certain; menace carried entirely by calm. Speaks English with a soft Colombian
accent.

**ROSA** — a Medellín barrio mother, around 40; dark hair pulled back; simple
cotton dress with an apron; warm, direct, unafraid; speaks English with a Colombian
accent. Appears in clip 6 only.

## THE SCRIPT

| # | Dur | Scene, dialogue, EYELINES | Craft job |

| 1 | 12s | **THE GATE / HOOK.** First frame, mute-legible: a small airplane mounted on top of a white ranch gate, HAZEL walking under it toward her lens (forward walk, §14). Fast, bright: "May, nineteen eighty-seven. Forbes just named Earth's richest men — and one of them sells cocaine. That plane flew his first shipment. Today, I'm having lunch with him." EYELINES: her eyes to lens throughout; one glance up at the plane on "that plane," back to lens. | Strange image + billionaire-criminal paradox inside the swipe window; the slow last line is the audience-knows contract, stated plainly |
| 2 | 10s | **THE GROUNDS.** Walking the ranch road, animals visible, staff moving in background. "His ranch has its own zoo. Four hippos — remember them." A HIPPO surfaces loudly in the lake beside her mid-step — she flinches, laughs one breath (embedded, §7): "Okay. He makes four hundred and twenty million dollars a week. There's so much cash in storage, rats eat ten percent of it." EYELINES: eyes to lens; snap to the hippo on the surface-splash, hold half a beat, back to lens. | Stat barrage told plainly + hippo PLANT + real reaction beat; §17 continuous hippo motion |
| 3 | 10s | **THE VERANDA — MEET PABLO.** Lunch table, fruit and coffee, staff at edges. PABLO (§32 lock) seated, relaxed, host-warm. *[Pablo]*, unhurried: "I built hundreds of houses for people who lived on the garbage dump. Schools. Soccer fields." (0.5s, small smile) "They call me the Robin Hood of Medellín." HAZEL, level, not smiling: "And the people who tell you no?" EYELINES: his eyes on her through his lines, one slow gesture toward the valley on "houses"; her eyes on him the whole clip — she does NOT look at the lens in this clip; his eyes return to hers on her question and hold. | The featured-figure intro; his charm played absolutely straight so clip 4 lands |
| 4 | 12s | **SILVER OR LEAD.** Same table, tighter. *[Pablo]*, calm, almost kind: "No one tells me no. Everyone accepts something." (0.5s) "Silver… or lead." A man appears at the veranda edge; Pablo rises, touches her shoulder in apology, steps away. HAZEL waits, then turns to her lens, hushed and quick: "Silver is money. Lead is a bullet. The last minister who came after him was murdered. Everyone knows. Nobody can prove it." EYELINES: his eyes hold hers through "Silver… or lead"; her eyes follow him as he walks away, and only when he is clearly distant do they come to the lens; one flick back toward him mid-aside, then back to lens. | The signature quote + its meaning explained in plain words (plain-language rule); whispered audience-knows dread, §2 restrained |
| 5 | 11s | **THE MONEY ROOM.** A storeroom: raw cash stacked in bricks to the ceiling. Pablo tosses her a rubber band — she catches it and wraps a loose brick herself (active, §12). HAZEL, to lens while wrapping: "He spends twenty-five hundred dollars a month on rubber bands." To him: "You could stop. Why don't you?" *[Pablo]*, amused, patting a stack once: "I offered to pay my country's whole national debt. Ten billion dollars. They said no." EYELINES: her eyes track the tossed band, then down to her hands while wrapping, up to HIM for her question; his eyes on her for the answer, one glance at the cash wall on "ten billion," back to her. | The most shareable fact of his life as dialogue + hands-on participant beat; cash only, never product |
| 6 | 11s | **THE BARRIO.** Golden afternoon, a poor Medellín street he built — kids playing on a concrete pitch, neighbors, a radio. ROSA (§32 lock), to Hazel, warm and certain: "Don Pablo built our whole street. God bless him." Hazel nods, holds a beat, walks a few steps, then quietly to lens, jokes gone: "The help is real. But he kills journalists. Judges. Policemen. This whole country lives between his silver and his lead." EYELINES: Rosa's eyes on Hazel; Hazel's eyes on Rosa through her line and the held beat after; only after turning away do her eyes come to the lens, and they stay there, steady, through the quiet piece. | The empathy core, never rushed: both truths said plainly back-to-back; "silver and lead" callback does the connecting work in literal words |
| 7 | 15s | **THE FEAR.** Interior, quieter, ceiling fan. HAZEL, direct: "February. Your partner Lehder — taken to an American prison. Are you afraid?" *[Pablo]* — the warmth stops. Level, slow: "I would rather have a grave in Colombia…" (0.5s) "…than a jail cell in the United States." He looks toward the window. She turns to lens, hushed: "Next month the Supreme Court kills the extradition treaty. He wins. For six years." EYELINES: her eyes on him for the question; his eyes hold hers, unblinking, through both halves of his line; on his window-turn her eyes go to the lens for the aside, one glance back at his profile on "he wins," back to lens. | His real recorded worldview as the tension peak; the owner-locked short aside (restraint, not irony) |
| 8 | 8s | **THE FAREWELL.** Dusk beginning, he walks her toward the gate, host to the end. HAZEL: "Last question. How does this end, Pablo?" *[Pablo]*, small smile, completely certain: "It doesn't. Colombia and I have an arrangement." He turns back toward the house. She watches him go — then her eyes settle on the lens. TWO FULL SECONDS, silent, nothing said. EYELINES: mutual eye contact through question and answer; her eyes follow his back as he walks away; then to lens for the silent hold — no other movement. | Owner-locked restraint beat: the silence carries it; beat 9 states the facts moments later |
| 9a | 13s | **THE WALK OUT — FUTURE-CAST.** She walks the drive toward the gate (forward walk, §14), long golden light, pace RISING: "Here's what he doesn't know. In two years, Forbes ranks him seventh-richest on Earth — and his men bomb a passenger plane. A hundred and ten people. Then a prison he builds himself. Jacuzzi. Football field. His own guards. Then he escapes. Hiding, he burns two million dollars to keep his daughter warm." EYELINES: eyes to lens, natural forward-walk glances ahead per §14. | The rest of his life, spoken plainly at urgent pace — energy UP for the final stretch; linear (her telling moves only forward), no visual flash-forward |
| 9b | 7s | **THE DATE.** She stops under the gate plane, slow and level: "December second, nineteen ninety-three. A rooftop in Medellín. One day after his forty-fourth birthday. His war killed thousands of people." EYELINES: eyes to lens, unmoving. | The ending + the cost, four plain sentences; the deliberate-slow contrast beat after 9a's speed |
| 10 | 15s | **OUTRO — THE HIPPOS.** Dusk, crickets, the ranch behind her. Quiet, plain: "Today, everything he built is gone. The money. The cartel. This ranch. Except the hippos. Nobody could move them — they escaped, and kept breeding. Over a hundred live wild in Colombia today. All from his four." (0.5s) Soft: "Hazel — out of time." EYELINES: eyes to lens throughout; one small glance back at the ranch on "this ranch," back to lens for everything after. | Owner-approved plain hippo payoff — the fact is the punchline, nothing to decode + sign-off |
