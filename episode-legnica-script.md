## 1. Historical research (owner's brief requires this before finalizing anything)

**Certain / well-supported:**
- Battle fought **April 9, 1241**, at Legnickie Pole (historically "Wahlstatt"), ~10 km from the town of Legnica (Liegnitz), Duchy of Silesia — part of the broader 1241 Mongol invasion of Europe.
- **Henry II the Pious**, Duke of Silesia, led a coalition of Polish and Moravian troops, feudal levies, and a small contingent from military orders (Teutonic Knights, Templars, Hospitallers).
- The Mongol force was commanded by **Baidar and Kadan** (a detachment of Batu and Subutai's western army) — a **diversionary force**, sent to stop Polish/Silesian reinforcements from reaching the main Mongol army in Hungary. The main battle there, **Mohi**, followed just two days later (April 11, 1241).
- Mongol tactics: mounted horse archery, a **feigned retreat** that drew European heavy cavalry out of formation in pursuit, then encirclement of the separated, disorganized force.
- Henry's army was broken; **Henry was killed while his position collapsed**, and the coalition force was effectively destroyed as a fighting unit.
- **Best-supported reconstruction of his death: he was cut down/overwhelmed while trying to escape the collapse (some accounts say with a small bodyguard), and beheaded; his head was raised on a lance/spear and paraded before the walls of Legnica to break the defenders' morale.** This is the version used in the script below.
- The Mongols withdrew from Central Europe in **1242** — driven by the death of Great Khan Ögedei in December 1241 and the succession crisis it triggered, **not** because of defeat at Legnica or Mohi.

**Disputed / NOT presented as fact on screen:**
- **Exact troop numbers and casualty counts are disputed and likely exaggerated in later medieval chronicles** (older sources claim tens of thousands on each side and figures like "nine sacks of ears" collected to count the dead). Modern historians consider the real forces far smaller on both sides. Per the owner's own instruction, **no specific troop numbers are stated in dialogue or on-screen text.**
- The blow-by-blow choreography of Henry's final seconds (who struck him, how many attackers, whether he was pulled from his horse first) is **not** established with certainty by any primary source — the script below is a **visually plausible cinematic reconstruction** consistent with the best-supported outline (overwhelmed while fleeing → beheaded → head displayed), not a claimed precise account.
- A later Polish legend (the "Life of St. Hedwig," written decades after) adds embellishments — a Mongol shouting "Run! Run!" in the defenders' own language, a poisonous-smoke banner — that most historians treat as legendary rather than verified fact. **Not used in this script.**

**On-screen dialogue** (Hazel↔Henry exchange, soldier shouts) is clearly **cinematic reconstruction, not a documented quotation** — consistent with how every other episode's NPC dialogue is handled.

**Pinned-comment draft:** "Legnica, April 9, 1241 — real battle, real date, real diversion from the Mongols' main invasion of Hungary (Mohi fell two days later). Henry II the Pious was killed in the collapse and beheaded; his head was displayed to Legnica's defenders.
