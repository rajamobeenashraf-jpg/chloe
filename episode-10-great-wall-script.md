## Fact verification (researched 2026-08-26, sources on file in session)
- **Tumu Crisis, 1449:** Emperor Zhengtong (Yingzong) captured alive by Oirat Mongol forces; after his 1457 restoration the court adopted a defensive wall strategy; sustained Ming wall-building from 1474.
- **Qi Jiguang (1528–1588):** commanded the Jizhen frontier from 1567/68; rebuilt this stretch with brick facing over rammed earth and 1,200+ hollow garrison watchtowers, Shanhaiguan→Changping.
- **Sticky-rice lime mortar:** real; 2010 Zhejiang University study confirmed amylopectin makes it stronger/more water-resistant than plain lime.
- **Labor force:** soldiers reassigned from patrol, convicts (wall labor in lieu of execution), civilians drafted under corvée.
- **Cost:** 1576 extension projected at 3.3 million ounces of silver — over three-quarters of annual state revenue. (Script says "this year's" in-world; the pinned comment carries the exact 1576 sourcing.)
- **Length/time:** Ming wall 8,851.8 km (2009 joint national survey), ten provinces, ~170 years of construction across many emperors.
- **Deaths:** no reliable Ming-specific toll; the famous "400,000 died" figure is a Qin-era estimate, itself disputed. "Bodies built into the wall" is a MYTH — no remains ever found in the masonry; the real fact is uncounted mass graves beside the wall. Script states the true version.
- **1644:** Wu Sangui opened Shanhaiguan Pass to Qing forces; the wall's strongest gate was opened, not breached.
