## Format notes — how this script relates to the standing rules

This is a **different structural device** from the Chloe-formula episodes (`episodes-2-4-scripts.md`, `episodes-5-9-scripts.md`, the Alexander script): those open directly in the past and never return to the present. This one is a **dream-frame**: modern bedroom → sleep → 1750 London → wakes in the modern bedroom again. Checked against the standing rules before writing:

- **OPENING LAW (linear, no flash-forwards/rewind cards)** — still satisfied. Nothing here jumps backward or previews an outcome; time runs forward the whole way (tonight → she falls asleep → the dream plays start-to-finish → she wakes later that night/morning). A dream is not a flashback card.
- **HAZEL name lock** — protagonist is HAZEL throughout, present day and 1750 alike.
- **V-mode / direct-address floor** — first-person selfie-lens the whole runtime; she talks to the audience or reacts into the lens at least every ~5s, same floor as every other episode.
- **CHARACTER_LOCK v5** — Hazel's face/appearance reference for any stills this script eventually needs.
- **Sign-off ritual** — the owner's specified ending ("Oh, thank God… It was just a dream." / "And the power's back.") is a complete, self-contained button and is used verbatim, per the brief. The usual "Hazel — out of time" sign-off is NOT appended here since it would double up on an already-landed final beat and wasn't asked for — flagging this as an open question for the owner rather than deciding it silently (see Open items below).
- **Caption system, ElevenLabs pacing references, per-beat eyeline** — all apply once this reaches prompt-writing, not at script stage. Flagged in Open items so they aren't forgotten.
- **Historical grounding** — no fabricated famous events or named historical figures here (ordinary household, not a documented incident), so no pinned-comment fact-check is required the way the other episodes need one; a short authenticity note is included below instead.

## NPC locks (write once, reuse verbatim once prompt-writing starts)

**AGNES** — the woman of the household, early-to-mid thirties; plain linen cap over dark hair, a coarse wool bodice and skirt, sleeves pushed back to the elbow, forearms reddened from fire and washing. Brisk, warm, unbothered by hardship because it's just Tuesday to her — treats Hazel's confusion as ordinary sleepiness, not as anything strange. Moves fast and economically; never explains a task before doing it.

**THOMAS** — Agnes's husband, mid-thirties, a chandler (candle-maker) by trade; lean, wiry-forearmed from dipping work, faint permanent tallow smell, plain brown coat and waistcoat, flat cap. Dry, quietly funny, unhurried even when rushing. Deliberate small irony built into his introduction and never spoken aloud: his entire livelihood is making the light Hazel's world has automated away.

**Setting**: a modest terraced house off a City of London side street — one hearth room doing double duty as kitchen/parlour, a small chandler's workshop Thomas walks to, a cobbled street outside. Palette: warm tallow-and-firelight ochre indoors, flat grey daylight outdoors, deep blue-black dusk for the coin scene lit only by one hand lantern.

## Flow (linear, one continuous night-into-night loop)

Modern bedroom (1–2) → falls asleep (2) → wakes in 1750, meets Agnes (3) → breakfast/cooking at the hearth (4) → ironing (5) → outside to Thomas's chandler shop, helps dip wicks (6) → food preservation back at the house (7) → daylight fades, street winds down (8) → outside at dusk, Thomas drops a coin, lantern search (9) → fireside, mending, drifts off (10) → wakes in her own bed, remembers, relief, power's back (11).

## Sound & palette

Modern bedroom: quiet room tone, the click of the plug, the drop into silence when power cuts, phone-charge chime returning at the very end (a clean, satisfying payoff sound). 1750 London: hearth crackle and pot-bubble indoors; street clatter, cart wheels, distant church bell by day; the dusk/coin scene drops to near-silence — just footsteps on cobbles and the lantern's own small creak, so the difficulty of seeing reads through sound too. Fireside mending closes on the fire's crackle alone, dying under her breathing as she drifts off.

## Authenticity note (for a pinned comment / description, if this goes to production)

Ordinary working household, not a named historical event — grounded in general daily life for an artisan family in mid-18th-century London: hearth cooking, flat irons heated on the fire, tallow-dipped candles as the chandler's trade, salting/pickling/root-cellar storage as the standard ways to keep food before refrigeration, and hand lanterns as the only portable light after dark. No specific claimed incident or named person — general social history rather than a single sourced anecdote, so it doesn't need the disputed/embellished-anecdote flag the other episodes carry.
