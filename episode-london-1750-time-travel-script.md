## NPC locks (write once, reuse verbatim once prompt-writing starts)

**AGNES** — the woman of the household, early-to-mid thirties; plain linen cap over dark hair, a coarse wool bodice and skirt, sleeves pushed back to the elbow, forearms reddened from fire and washing. Brisk, warm, unbothered by hardship because it's just Tuesday to her — treats Hazel's confusion as ordinary sleepiness, not as anything strange. Moves fast and economically; never explains a task before doing it.

**THOMAS** — Agnes's husband, mid-thirties, a chandler (candle-maker) by trade; lean, wiry-forearmed from dipping work, faint permanent tallow smell, plain brown coat and waistcoat, flat cap. Dry, quietly funny, unhurried even when rushing. Deliberate small irony built into his introduction and never spoken aloud: his entire livelihood is making the light Hazel's world has automated away.

**BESS** — their daughter, about seven; patched pinafore over a plain shift, hair in a single loose braid, a bit grubby at the cuffs. Blunt and curious in the specific way small children are around a stranger who "talks funny" — she's the character who notices Hazel is odd out loud, which is funnier coming from a child than it would be from an adult, and is defused with a laugh rather than an explanation.

**Setting**: a modest terraced house off a City of London side street — one hearth room doing double duty as kitchen/parlour, a small chandler's workshop Thomas walks to, a shared street pump around the corner, a cobbled street outside. Palette: warm tallow-and-firelight ochre indoors, flat grey daylight outdoors, deep blue-black dusk for the coin scene lit only by one hand lantern.

## Flow (linear, one continuous night-into-night loop)

Modern bedroom, hook + phone charge + power out + tired (1–3) → falls asleep (3) → wakes in 1750, meets Agnes (4) → breakfast/cooking at the hearth, meets Bess (5) → ironing (6) → fetching water at the street pump (7) → outside to Thomas's chandler shop, helps dip wicks (8) → food preservation + church-bell noon (9) → daylight fades, street winds down (10) → outside at dusk, Thomas drops a coin, lantern search (11) → fireside, mending, drifts off (12) → wakes in her own bed, remembers, relief, power's back (13).

## Sound & palette

Modern bedroom: quiet room tone, the click of the plug, the drop into silence when power cuts, phone-charge chime returning at the very end (a clean, satisfying payoff sound). 1750 London: hearth crackle and pot-bubble indoors; street clatter, cart wheels, the pump's iron creak and splash, overlapping women's chatter in the queue, a distant church bell at noon; the dusk/coin scene drops to near-silence — just footsteps on cobbles and the lantern's own small creak, so the difficulty of seeing reads through sound too. Fireside mending closes on the fire's crackle alone, dying under her breathing as she drifts off.

## Authenticity note (for a pinned comment / description, if this goes to production)

Ordinary working household, not a named historical event — grounded in general daily life for an artisan family in mid-18th-century London: hearth cooking, flat irons heated on the fire, tallow-dipped candles as the chandler's trade (beeswax genuinely was the pricier, cleaner-smelling alternative reserved for those who could afford it), salting/pickling/root-cellar storage as the standard ways to keep food before refrigeration, communal street pumps as the ordinary way most households got water (no household taps), church bells as the everyday way ordinary people told the time before personal clocks or watches were affordable, and hand lanterns as the only portable light after dark — losing a coin after dark was a real, meaningful loss, not a minor inconvenience. No specific claimed incident or named person — general social history rather than a single sourced anecdote, so it doesn't need the disputed/embellished-anecdote flag the other episodes carry.
